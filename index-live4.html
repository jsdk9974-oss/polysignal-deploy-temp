What cannot be measured automatically yet

Robotics has no usable public database. There is no Hugging Face equivalent for robots: production announcements, order books and deployments live only in press releases. That ranking will be kept by hand, from the daily watch, with a verification date on every line — and saying so is more honest than manufacturing a league table.

Chipmakers' accounts are public but heavy. Nvidia, AMD and Intel file their figures with the US regulator, reachable by API without a key. That is the next source to wire in: it will give a ranking by actual revenue rather than by press estimate.

Private lab valuations cannot be verified. No open database publishes them. They will stay displayed as reported figures, with their source and their date — never as facts.

Were we right?

The only page that counts. A good forecaster is not the one who is always right: it is the one whose “70%” comes true about seven times out of ten.

DomainResolvedBrierMean error

The Brier score is the mean squared error: 0 is perfect, 0.25 is what answering “50%” to everything gets you. Professional superforecasters run around 0.08.

The mean error is the average distance between the stated probability and the outcome, in points. More intuitive than Brier, but it forgives large misses: read the two together.

None of these questions exists on a prediction market — that is the point of the site. So the comparison isn't a price: it's the "reference" row below, the score of a strategy that states the base rate and reads nothing. If we don't beat that, the whole pipeline is worthless.

How this is made

Five steps. Wording and estimation are always machine; for finance, crypto/ETF and mergers, spotting is now adopted automatically once a candidate clears a mechanical filter and an adversarial AI review — no systematic human step there. Everything else is a scheduled script, readable, with every decision traced.

01Spotting

For science and health, the watch reads international press in the language of the country concerned, Hacker News and lab announcements. For finance, crypto/ETF and mergers, it reads official filings on a fixed schedule instead (SEC filings, FTC merger notices) — no press. Either way, it proposes candidate questions.

02Wording

Every question gets a deadline, an objective criterion and a designated resolution source before the estimate. Without those three, it does not ship.

03Estimation

The model gathers the sources, produces a probability and the reasoning behind it. The reasoning is what makes the page worth reading.

04Publication

One page per question, and a snapshot of the sources archived on the day of the estimate — so that a dead link six months later does not make the forecast unverifiable.

05Resolution

At the deadline the designated source is consulted, the verdict recorded, the calibration recomputed. This is the step almost nobody does.

What this site is not. Not financial advice, not medical advice. Health questions are about agency decisions and clinical-trial endpoints — never about what a treatment will do for any particular person.

What we commit to. Publishing the misses as visibly as the hits, never changing an estimate without timestamping the change, and showing the score even when it is bad.

Where the numbers come from. Press: RSS feeds, queried in the language of the country concerned, each article carrying a source tier. Company figures: official filings where they exist, otherwise press, cited and dated. No prediction-market odds: none of these questions has a market.
